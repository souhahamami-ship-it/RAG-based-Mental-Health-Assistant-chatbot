Grounding techniques are simple exercises that help bring attention back to the present moment when someone feels overwhelmed, anxious, or disconnected from their surroundings.

One common method involves the senses: noticing five things you can see, four things you can touch, three things you can hear, two things you can smell, and one thing you can taste. This shifts attention away from distressing thoughts and toward immediate physical experience.

Other grounding approaches include holding a cold object, pressing feet firmly into the floor and noticing the sensation, naming the current date, location, and time out loud, or describing a neutral object in detail.

Grounding techniques are generally most helpful for moments of acute distress or anxiety rather than as a long-term treatment on their own. They can be a useful tool alongside other forms of support, such as therapy, for people who experience frequent anxiety or distress.
