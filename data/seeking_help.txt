It is not always obvious when a difficult period has crossed into something that benefits from professional support. Some general signals include: symptoms lasting more than two weeks, difficulty functioning at work, school, or in relationships, withdrawal from people or activities that used to matter, and using substances to cope with emotions.

Other signals include persistent physical symptoms without a clear medical cause, such as ongoing fatigue, appetite changes, or sleep disruption tied to emotional distress, as well as any thoughts of self-harm or feeling that life is not worth continuing.

A mental health professional, such as a psychologist, psychiatrist, licensed counselor, or clinical social worker, can help assess what is happening and recommend appropriate support, which might include therapy, medication, or both. A primary care doctor is also a reasonable starting point and can provide referrals.

Seeking help is not a sign of failure, and earlier support is often associated with better outcomes. If there is any concern about immediate safety, contacting emergency services or a crisis line takes priority over any other step.
