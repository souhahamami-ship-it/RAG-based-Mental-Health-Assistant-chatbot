Depression is more than ordinary sadness. It involves a persistent low mood, loss of interest or pleasure in most activities, and can affect sleep, appetite, energy, concentration, and self-worth for two weeks or longer.

Depression can occur without an obvious trigger, and it does not reflect weakness or a personal failing. It is one of the most common mental health conditions and is generally treatable.

Common signs include feeling persistently down or empty, losing interest in things once enjoyed, changes in sleep or appetite, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, feelings of worthlessness or guilt, and in more severe cases, thoughts of death or self-harm.

Support options include talking with a qualified mental health professional, evidence-based therapies such as cognitive behavioral therapy, and in some cases medication prescribed by a doctor. Reaching out for support is a reasonable first step, and it often helps to also involve a trusted person who can check in over time.
